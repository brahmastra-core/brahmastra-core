# Mathematical Theory: Post-Quantum Lattice Security & Error-Tolerant Formulation

## 1. Lattice Cryptographic Foundation
A discrete sub-group $\Lambda \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ generated by linearly independent basis vectors $\mathbf{B} = \{\mathbf{b}_1, \dots, \mathbf{b}_n\}$ defines our multi-dimensional configuration space:
$$\Lambda(\mathbf{B}) = \left\{ \sum_{i=1}^n x_i \mathbf{b}_i : x_i \in \mathbb{Z} \right\}$$

### 1.1 Shortest Vector Problem (SVP)
Given basis $\mathbf{B}$, finding a non-zero vector $\mathbf{v} \in \Lambda$ such that $\|\mathbf{v}\| \le \gamma \cdot \lambda_1(\Lambda)$ where $\lambda_1(\Lambda) = \min_{\mathbf{x} \in \Lambda \setminus \{\mathbf{0}\}} \|\mathbf{x}\|$ represents an NP-hard problem for polynomial approximation factors $\gamma$.

### 1.2 Learning With Errors (LWE) Formulation
Let $n, q \in \mathbb{N}$ and $\chi$ be an error distribution over $\mathbb{Z}_q$. For secret key vector $\mathbf{s} \in \mathbb{Z}_q^n$, independent samples are computed as:
$$(\mathbf{a}_i, b_i) = (\mathbf{a}_i, \langle \mathbf{a}_i, \mathbf{s} \rangle + e_i) \in \mathbb{Z}_q^n \times \mathbb{Z}_q$$
where $\mathbf{a}_i \leftarrow \mathcal{U}(\mathbb{Z}_q^n)$ and $e_i \leftarrow \chi$. Distinguishing these samples from uniformly random pairs is bounded by worst-case hardness of lattice shortest vector problems.

## 2. Quantum Resistance Proof Against Shor's Decomposition
### 2.1 Failure of Abelian Hidden Subgroup Solvers
Shor's algorithm efficiently computes discrete logarithms and prime factorizations in polynomial time $\mathcal{O}((\log N)^3)$ by exploiting the periodic abelian group structure:
$$f(x) = f(x + r) \implies \text{Quantum Fourier Transform finds period } r$$
In lattice-based constructions, the underlying structure relies on non-abelian or non-periodic multi-dimensional geometries ($n \ge 1024$). No polynomial-time Quantum Fourier Transform or period-finding mechanism exists that collapses the LWE error lattice into an orthogonal state.

### 2.2 Quantum Hardness Reduction (Worst-Case to Average-Case)
By Regev’s quantum reduction, any quantum polynomial-time adversary capable of solving LWE with non-negligible advantage $\epsilon$ can solve GapSVP and SIVP (Shortest Independent Vector Problem) in the worst case with:
$$\mathcal{O}(n / \alpha) \text{ approximation factor}$$
where $\alpha \cdot q > \sqrt{n}$. Thus, under 256-bit post-quantum parameters, security is unconditionally preserved against polynomial quantum adversaries.
